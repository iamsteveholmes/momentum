# Planning Lens — How Nornspun Sprints Get Planned, and What Planning Optimizes For

**Date:** 2026-08-02
**Role:** One of several parallel discovery lenses investigating why sprint-2026-07-13 (12/12 stories done, end-gate approved) shipped an app the developer says "couldn't do even the simplest things." This lens examines the planning layer only: coverage plans, plan-gate artifacts, the sprint-planning skill's mechanics, wave/dependency design, planning cost, and roadmap fidelity. Build execution, QA, and retro-audit findings are other lenses' territory except where directly load-bearing for a planning-layer claim.

---

## Executive summary

1. **The unit of planning is the story, not the feature — mechanically confirmed.** `sprint-planning/workflow.md` (both v0.31.0, which planned sprint-2026-07-13, and the current v0.32.0) contains zero references to `.momentum/features.json` — grep for `features.json` across the workflow returns nothing. Story selection (Step 2) is "select 2-8 stories"; the backlog is grouped by `epic_slug` for display only.
2. **A feature-level, user-capability registry already exists and is orphaned from planning.** `.momentum/features.json` phrases every feature's `acceptance_condition` exactly as "what will the user be able to do" ("A developer can describe the outcomes of a game session to Urd in 10 conversational turns...", `last_verified: 2026-06-16`). It was last written by `momentum:assessment` (ASR-006 reconciliation), never by sprint-planning. Its `session-capture` and `living-memory` entries' `stories` arrays contain **none** of the 12 stories sprint-2026-07-13 actually built, and its `session-capture.acceptance_condition` still describes the rejected "binding confirmation before persistence" gate that this very sprint's Fork 1 removed.
3. **No planning artifact anywhere in the corpus asks "what will the user be able to do after this sprint?" as a selection or gating criterion.** Confirmed by targeted search across `workflow.md`, all 5 `coverage-plan.md` files, and both plan-gate documents. The plan gate *does* synthesize a plain-language "what this sprint does" narrative — but only in Step 7, as a post-hoc summary of already-selected stories, not as a Step-1/2 selection filter.
4. **The sprint's own "integration scenario" — the mechanism specifically designed to catch story-vs-feature composition gaps — never touches the client app.** Coverage-plan.md's Scenario A (session capture, close, naming, recap) composes 4 stories and is verified entirely via `curl`/`bash` against the backend (`platforms: [host]` only, no android/desktop client driven). Mechanical cause: `verification-standard.md`'s routing table hard-maps `change_type: backend → curl`, and every story composed into Scenario A is `change_type: backend`.
5. **Wave sequencing is 100% file-conflict-and-dependency mechanics, never user-value framing.** Every wave-rationale note recorded for sprint-2026-07-13 cites touched-file overlaps or `depends_on` order ("no touched-file overlap among the four," "both touch urd.py/verdandi.py... so the two are serialized"). None cites a user-observable milestone.
6. **Sprint composition is an exact, same-order copy of the adopted roadmap's Sprint A** — but the roadmap explicitly cuts sprints along a "backend dependency spine" and defers full user-observable value ("Friends & Family Ready") to Sprint E, five sprints out. By the roadmap's own design, sprint-2026-07-13 was never intended to be usable end-to-end on its own.
7. **The AI-mechanical planning process is fast — the opposite of what "lots of time planning" suggests if read literally.** Sprint-2026-07-13's full planning arc (story selection → rendered plan gate) took 89 minutes of wall-clock time (10:56am–12:25pm PDT, 21 commits, 1,142 transcript lines).
8. **The real time cost is an invisible, multi-day-to-multi-week calendar gap between plan-ready and build-activate**, present in every sprint sampled: 7 days (sprint-2026-07-13), 18 days (sprint-2026-06-18), 3–8 days (sprint-2026-05-30). Build execution itself, once started, is consistently fast (2–3 days).
9. **The gap is conditional on story mix, not universal.** Sprint-2026-06-18 (all `app-ui` stories) produced a coverage plan that is genuinely user-journey-shaped and UI-driven end to end. The curl-only blind spot is specific to backend-heavy, two-repo sprints — exactly the shape of sprint-2026-07-13.
10. **The plan gate discloses the shape of the gap without naming its consequence.** It states "Backend contracts are unexercised... they run for real only as waves 3–6 land" and the verification-method split ("6 smoke, 3 curl, 3 bash") — the information was present, but never translated into "the real chat conversation will not be driven through the app this sprint."

## Body

### 1. Unit of planning: story, confirmed against the one artifact that says otherwise

`skills/momentum/skills/sprint-planning/workflow.md` (v0.31.0 — the version active on 2026-07-13, per plugin version-bump commit dates: `f1f68a0` 0.31.0 on 2026-07-06, `0b9d257` 0.32.0 on 2026-07-30) drives the entire planning arc. Its Step 1 recommends stories from the backlog weighted by "priority field... master plan alignment... dependency readiness... recency of related PRD edits" (workflow.md:139-143). Step 2 is a hard `<ask>Select 2-8 stories for this sprint...</ask>` (workflow.md:197). Backlog display groups stories `by epic_slug` (workflow.md:144) — an organizational label, not a completion unit with its own acceptance test.

Search executed: `grep -n -i "features.json\|feature_slug\|epic_slug" workflow.md` across both v0.31.0 and v0.32.0 caches. Result: zero hits for `features.json` in either version; the only `feature_*` hits are `feature_state_transition` fields sourced from retro-handoff ledger payloads (a different mechanism, see §4), and `epic_slug` used purely for backlog grouping and story-stub creation.

Meanwhile `.momentum/features.json` (`/Users/steve/projects/nornspun/.momentum/features.json`, 15 entries, mtime 2026-07-13 10:55 — unchanged by the sprint that ran that same day) is structurally exactly the artifact the mission brief's question is asking for. Every entry has:
- `acceptance_condition`: a plain-language "X can do Y" sentence (e.g. `session-capture`: *"A developer can describe the outcomes of a game session to Urd in 10 conversational turns and see living memory updated with session events, NPC status changes, and at least one divergence from the adventure plan — with a binding confirmation before persistence."*)
- `status`: not-started / partial / working
- `stories`: the story slugs that compose it
- `last_verified`: a date stamp

`git log --oneline --all -- .momentum/features.json` shows its one substantive content commit is `0b6b064 chore(momentum): reconcile features.json to ASR-006 (AVFL-verified) for the 7 MVP clusters` — an **assessment** artifact, not a sprint-planning one. Sprint-planning neither reads it to select stories, nor writes it back after a sprint to record that a feature's acceptance condition moved closer to true. Concretely: `session-capture.stories` = `['urds-post-session-capture', 'binding-ritual-context-summarization']` and `living-memory.stories` = 9 slugs, none of which include `episode-transcript-persistence-store-a`, `episode-auto-segmentation-boundary-model`, `episode-close-summarization-store-b`, `session-naming-two-message-close`, or `session-counter-live-update-on-capture-close` — the five stories that are precisely what those two features are about, and that this sprint built. The feature registry's own `notes` field for `session-capture` still says *"binding-ritual-context-summarization is a PHANTOM slug (no .md file) — purge in story-delta"* — the registry is aware it is stale but nothing in the planning cycle repairs it.

**What this means:** the practice already possesses the correct instrument for "will the user be able to do X after this sprint" — it's just disconnected. Feature tracking and sprint planning are two parallel, non-communicating systems in this codebase.

### 2. No planning artifact asks the user-value question as a gate

Searches executed (case-insensitive, across `workflow.md`, all 5 `coverage-plan.md` files, `plan-gate-decision.md`, and `.momentum/plan-gate-sprint-2026-06-18.md`):
- `"what will|able to (do|use)|end.to.end user|user (will|can) be able|complete feature|whole feature|feature complete"` — zero hits that constitute a planning question (the only "end-to-end" hits describe a UI navigation return-leg or a migration retirement scenario, not a gating question).
- `"unit of planning|per.feature|feature.level"` — the only hits are noise from an unrelated E2E validator's "Per-Feature Results" table header (per-story-file, not per-product-feature) inside an old transcript.

The plan gate does produce a plain-language, user-facing summary — the sprint-2026-07-13 gate's hero section reads: *"Builds the memory foundation: every conversation is durably recorded per campaign, organized into episodes, and summarized on close — while the capture flow loses its rejected 'Shall I bind?' gate and the campaign picker becomes real."* (`.momentum/handoffs/sprint-2026-07-13-plan-gate.html`). This is genuinely readable and honest. But it is generated in **Step 7** — after story selection (Step 2), fleshing-out (Step 3), contract authoring (Step 3.5), and Gherkin generation (Step 4) are already locked. It functions as a summary of a plan already made, never as the test used to decide what belongs in the plan.

### 3. The coverage-plan's own "integration scenario" mechanism is capped at the HTTP layer for backend-heavy sprints

`coverage-plan.md`'s stated purpose (workflow.md:406-430, Phase B) is explicitly to catch composition gaps: *"Never validate in isolation what an integrated scenario already exercises."* Its instruction for scenario descriptions even says to write "what the user does and what they observe" (workflow.md:419). For sprint-2026-07-13, Scenario A reads as a real user journey: *"a Spinner creates a fresh campaign, narrates a game session to Urd across several chat turns, and signals the end..."* (`.momentum/sprints/sprint-2026-07-13/coverage-plan.md:9-11`).

But the scenario's own text names its actual test surface: *"Every step is driven through the public HTTP surface (`/health`, `POST /api/campaigns`, `POST /api/chat`...)"* (coverage-plan.md:16-17). Cross-checked against `.momentum/sprints/index.json`'s `story_assignments`, the four stories Scenario A discharges carry `verification_method` values `curl, curl, bash, bash` — never `smoke` (the only method that drives the actual client). The two flagship capture-close stories' contracts state this candidly in their own headers:

> `episode-close-summarization-store-b` Dev Notes: *"Verification advisory = `curl` — the routing table (verification-standard.md §1) maps `backend` → `curl`, and the behavior IS curl-observable end-to-end... No override needed, so none is authored."*

> `session-naming-two-message-close.smoke.sh` header: `platforms: [host]` — the harness for the sprint's centerpiece "two-message close, session names" behavior runs against the backend process only, never against Android or Desktop.

`verification-standard.md` §1 (Method Routing Table) is the mechanical root cause: `change_type: backend → curl` is an unconditional mapping applied at contract-authoring time (Step 3.5), independent of whether the story's actual behavior is something a user experiences exclusively through the client chat UI. The routing decision is made from *which files a story touches*, not *where in the product a user encounters its behavior*. Because Scenario A composes only `backend`-typed stories, its "integration" ceiling is the HTTP layer by construction — no amount of scenario-writing craft inside coverage-plan.md can lift it past the method each component story was mechanically assigned.

The one story that does drive the real client for this feature area — `episode-transcript-persistence-store-a` (`smoke`, `platforms: [host, android]`) — is a **separate dedicated-run story**, not part of Scenario A, and its `.feature` scenarios test restart-survival and campaign isolation, not the conversational capture/close/naming/recap flow. **No artifact in this sprint drives the actual multi-turn Urd conversation, through the real chat UI, end to end.** The closest thing — `session-counter-live-update-on-capture-close` — is `smoke`/dedicated-run and sits at wave 7 (the terminal wave), verifying only that a counter number increments, not the conversation content leading up to it.

### 4. Wave/dependency design is pure file-conflict-and-dependency mechanics

`workflow.md` Step 5 instructs: *"Check for `touches` path overlaps across stories... flag as merge conflict risk. Stories with overlapping touches should be in different waves"* (workflow.md:870-874), and separately builds the wave graph strictly from `depends_on` fields (workflow.md:876-882). No step evaluates "does this wave, once merged, give the user something new to do."

The recorded wave rationale for sprint-2026-07-13 (`.momentum/sprints/index.json`, `planning.waves[].notes`) is 100% consistent with this:
- Wave 2: *"Parallel — no touched-file overlap among the four (exchange-id is backend; the three client stories touch disjoint files)."*
- Wave 3: *"backend-active-campaign-name-injection-fix merges FIRST within this wave... episode-transcript-persistence-store-a rebases after and preserves the name-not-UUID fix... (both touch urd.py/verdandi.py)."*
- Wave 5: *"Store B alone — overlaps session-naming on urd.py/memory.py/migrations (hidden by inconsistent path prefixes in touches), so the two are serialized."*

Every wave-boundary decision is expressed in terms of shared files and dependency edges. None is expressed in terms of "after this wave, a Spinner can do X they couldn't before." The one place a user-facing framing appears at all is the plan gate's post-hoc per-story `one_line_value` field (a renderer-synthesized summary, Step 7) — again downstream of wave construction, not an input to it.

### 5. Roadmap fidelity is real, but the roadmap itself defers user value past every individual sprint

`docs/planning-artifacts/mvp-roadmap-sprints-a-e-2026-07-09.md` "Sprint A — Memory foundation (12 stories)" lists exactly: `migrate-desktopapp-tests-from-kotlin-test-to-kotest, exchange-id-rename-across-norn-agents, episode-transcript-persistence-store-a, episode-auto-segmentation-boundary-model, episode-close-summarization-store-b, session-naming-two-message-close, urds-post-session-capture, session-counter-live-update-on-capture-close, campaign-picker-get-campaigns-client-repo, backend-active-campaign-name-injection-fix, urd-threshold-flowrow-inline-prose-fix, norn-greeting-capability-menu-trim`. `sprints/index.json`'s `active.stories` for sprint-2026-07-13 is the identical list, identical order. This is not loose alignment — it is an exact 1:1 copy.

The roadmap document is explicit about why it is sliced this way: *"Dependency spine is sequential: Store A → Store B → capture flows → prep surfaces → re-entry/ledger. Sprints are cut along that spine"* and labels Sprint A *"Backend-heavy; two-repo"* (mvp-roadmap-sprints-a-e-2026-07-09.md:16, 23). Its MVP gate — the only genuinely user-outcome-shaped statement in the document — is *"Campaign init → adventure ingestion → session prep → post-session capture → living memory → campaign identity — 'Friends & Family Ready'"* — and that gate is not scheduled to close until **Sprint E**, four sprints after the one under study (mvp-roadmap-sprints-a-e-2026-07-09.md:7-9, 52-54).

This matters for interpreting "couldn't do even the simplest things": by the roadmap's own explicit design, sprint-2026-07-13 was never supposed to be a complete, usable increment standing alone — it is one-fifth of a backend-dependency chain toward a milestone still several sprints away. That is a defensible engineering sequencing choice (you cannot build episode summarization before the transcript store exists). But nothing in the plan gate, the roadmap, or the coverage plan states the corollary plainly to the developer at approval time: *"this sprint alone will not make memory feel like it works when you open the app."* The absence is the same absence as finding 3 — the gap between "story passes its contract" and "user can do a thing" is real, present, and mechanically explainable, but never surfaced as an explicit expectation-setting statement.

### 6. Planning cost: fast mechanics, slow calendar gaps

Git commit timestamps for sprint-2026-07-13 (`git log --format="%h %ci %s"`, filtered to the sprint slug) show all 21 planning commits — from `fb7eed0` "select sprint-2026-07-13 stories per MVP roadmap Sprint A" through `7da4789` "render sprint-2026-07-13 visual plan gate" — landing between **10:56am and 12:25pm PDT on 2026-07-13**: 89 minutes. The corresponding session transcript (`cd4e9d0e-287d-4def-941e-34ca1ff47607.jsonl`) shows 1,142 lines / 2.95MB timestamped `2026-07-13` (17:42–19:26 UTC = 10:42am–12:26pm PDT — matching the commit window almost exactly) out of that file's total 1,494 lines — i.e., **96% of that session's transcript volume is the planning arc itself, compressed into under 2 hours.**

The next commits against this sprint are `ddf6422` "activate sprint-2026-07-13" and `5359e55` "conduct handoff," both timestamped **2026-07-20, 14:21–14:23 PDT** — **7 days later**. `git log --since=2026-07-13 --until=2026-07-21` shows zero other nornspun-repo commits in that window, and the nornspun Claude Code session file listing (`ls -la ~/.claude/projects/-Users-steve-projects-nornspun/*.jsonl`) shows no session at all between the 2026-07-13 planning session and 2026-07-20 (files bracketing the gap: `cd4e9d0e` ends its Jul-13 activity at 19:26 UTC that day, and its next timestamped line is 2026-07-20T21:19). Build then proceeded 2026-07-21 through 2026-07-22 (2,333 + 1,346 = 3,679 transcript lines, 4.82MB + 3.04MB across those two days) — roughly 2 days of build for 12 stories, once started.

The same pattern recurs, at different scale, in every other sprint whose commit history is legible:

| Sprint | Plan → activate gap | Activate → build-start gap | Build duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| sprint-2026-05-25 (2 stories) | same day (~3.5h) | same day | ~1 day (merged next morning) |
| sprint-2026-05-30 (15 stories) | 3 days (May 30 → Jun 2) | 5 days to runbook (Jun 2 → Jun 7) | ~2 days (Jun 7 runbook → Jun 8 end-gate) |
| sprint-2026-06-18 (8 stories) | same day (~2h19m) | **18 days** (Jun 18 → Jul 6 contract freeze) | ~2.5 days (Jul 6 → Jul 8 end-gate approved) |
| sprint-2026-07-13 (12 stories) | 7 days (plan-gate rendered → activation) | immediate | ~2 days (Jul 21 → Jul 22) |

The pattern: **the AI-mechanical planning workflow itself is never the long pole** — it is bounded (under 2.5 hours observed in every sampled case) and its cost scales mildly with story count. The developer-reported "lots of time planning" correlates far more plausibly with the **multi-day-to-multi-week calendar gaps** surrounding plan sign-off and build kickoff — gaps that leave zero trace in any git commit or Claude Code transcript, and that this lens cannot attribute to a specific cause (see Open Questions).

### 7. The gap is conditional, not universal — sprint-2026-06-18 is the counter-example that sharpens the finding

Sprint-2026-06-18's `coverage-plan.md` covers 8 stories, all `app-ui` change_type (a single-repo, client-only sprint — the pre-cursor "App Shell + Campaign Picker" sprint). Its Scenario A ("Create a campaign in the picker and switch back to it") is written and verified as a genuine UI walkthrough — *"the Spinner lands on the campaign picker, taps 'Start a new campaign', creates a campaign with Urd, lands in the workspace..."* — and every story in that sprint routes to `smoke` (build + launch + drive + human visual review), because every story's `change_type` is `app-ui`, which the routing table maps to `smoke`, not `curl`.

This confirms the mechanism precisely: the coverage-plan authoring process is not incapable of user-journey-shaped, UI-driven integration testing — it produces exactly that when the underlying stories are UI stories. The gap in sprint-2026-07-13 is a direct, mechanical consequence of that sprint's story mix being **two-repo and backend-heavy** (as the roadmap itself labels it), combined with a routing table that assigns verification depth per-story by touched-file layer rather than by where the user encounters the behavior. Cross-repo composition (backend logic the client must render and act on) is exactly the case the current routing table cannot express as an integration test.

### 8. The plan gate discloses the shape of the problem without stating its consequence

The sprint-2026-07-13 plan gate's own "Top Risks" section states, verbatim: *"Backend contracts are unexercised — the store contracts were authored against a spine that doesn't exist yet; they run for real only as waves 3–6 land. Contract drift surfaces at conduct, not before."* Its "Defaulted to standards" collapsible states the verification-method breakdown explicitly: *"6 smoke (.feature), 3 curl, 3 bash."* This is honest, checkable information, present in the artifact the developer reviewed and signed.

What it does not do is connect that breakdown to its user-facing consequence: nowhere does the gate say "the capture-close-naming-recap conversation itself will only be checked via raw HTTP calls this sprint, not by anyone driving the actual chat screen." The information needed to predict the outcome was disclosed in mechanical terms (method-routing counts); the inferential leap from "3 curl, 3 bash" to "the core feature won't be proven to work in the app" was left to the reader.

## Counter-evidence & falsifiability

- **Roadmap fidelity argues against a "planning is careless" reading.** The 12-story, same-order match to the adopted roadmap shows planning executed a deliberately sequenced technical plan faithfully, not an ad hoc grab-bag. The failure mode identified here is structural (routing-table mechanics, feature-registry disconnection), not negligence.
- **Sprint-2026-06-18 shows the coverage-plan mechanism can and does produce genuinely user-journey, UI-driven verification** when the story mix is UI-heavy. This is not a story that "planning never thinks about the user" — it thinks about the user exactly as well as the verification-method routing table lets it, and that table is keyed on code layer, not user-visibility.
- **The plan gate is not silent.** It explicitly names the "backend contracts are unexercised until waves land" risk and the verification-method split. A developer reading closely had the raw facts needed to predict the gap identified in §3, even though the artifact doesn't spell out the consequence in plain language.
- **The developer had 7 days to review, modify, or re-run AVFL on this plan** (the M/R paths exist precisely for this) and instead approved as-is, per-fork verdicts written per the anti-rubber-stamp mechanism recorded in `plan-gate-decision.md`. This complicates a purely "the planning skill is broken" narrative — whatever happened during that 7-day window is also part of why the gap wasn't caught before build, and this lens has no visibility into it.
- **What would falsify the core "story not feature" finding:** if `features.json` were found to be read or written anywhere in the sprint-planning workflow (checked: absent in both v0.31.0 and v0.32.0); if any wave-rationale note or coverage-plan scenario had explicitly gated on a user-milestone rather than file/dependency mechanics (checked across all recorded wave notes for sprint-2026-07-13: none found); if any cross-repo integration scenario in the corpus had been verified via a client-driving harness rather than curl/bash (checked: none found in sprint-2026-07-13; the one cross-repo composed scenario present is curl-only).

## Open questions

- **What actually happened during the 7-day (sprint-2026-07-13) and 18-day (sprint-2026-06-18) gaps between plan-ready and build-activate?** No nornspun git commits and no nornspun Claude Code session transcripts exist for either window. This lens cannot determine whether the developer was reviewing the plan gate outside of a tracked session, attending to competing priorities, or something else — the practice currently has no instrumentation that would record developer-side review time separately from AI-mechanical planning time.
- **Did momentum:conduct's actual per-story dev/QA/AVFL-on-merge/E2E phases catch or miss the curl-only blind spot left by planning's Scenario A** before end-gate approval? That is squarely a Build/QA/E2E lens question, but it is the direct downstream consequence of the planning-layer gap identified here, and the two lenses' findings should be read together.
- **Did the developer, during the 7-day gate review window, actually read and register the "6 smoke, 3 curl, 3 bash" disclosure** and its implication, or did the plan gate's information density bury a signal that was technically present? Unknowable from artifacts alone.
- **Did the pattern hold in the earliest sprints (04-05 through 05-01), before `coverage-plan.md` existed as a practice artifact** (it first appears at sprint-2026-05-25)? This lens sampled 05-25, 05-30, 06-18, and 07-13 in depth; the four April sprints were not examined to the same depth and may show a different (likely less structured) relationship between planning and user value.
