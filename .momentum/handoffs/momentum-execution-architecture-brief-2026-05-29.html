02The central question — unit of work

Your reframe (story vs. sprint vs. epic, from the HITL who plans then evaluates) is where the genuine, unresolved tradeoff lives. Execution grain is settled; the HITL gate grain is not.

STORYper-gate, worktree-held

Majority rejects; dissent defends. Lowest cognitive load per gate (one self-contained diff); change stays on a worktree until you approve, so the "final-merge" worry dissolves; existence proof = quick-fix; maps 1:1 to bd claim; directly relieves your stated planning pain. The one load-bearing objection: it defeats cross-story integration AVFL — but that assumes integration grain must equal planning grain (see dissent).

SPRINTthe modal + decided answer

One coherent curated evaluation; AVFL-on-merge catches cross-story contradictions; frozen+subtract-only means scope approved = scope verified; five skills already built around it. But: planning a 2–8 story batch is genuinely complex (your flagged pain); partly propped by sunk-cost gravity; its plan-gate weakness is mitigated only by the canvas epic — which doesn't exist yet.

EPICdismissed on a FALSE premise

The council waved it off as "relitigates the backward-looking epic." Verified false: DEC-034 defines a finite-lived epic as forward-looking with a real acceptance_condition. You evaluate one complete feature ("did it land?") — more meaningful than "is this changeset correct?" — with the fewest gate instances. Cons: worst latency; one blocked story stalls closure; no epic FSM/retro exists. Key collapse: for small finite-lived epics (3–5 stories), epic-as-unit and small-sprint-as-unit may be the same thing — and epic is then strictly better (it carries an acceptance_condition the sprint lacks).

DYNAMIC BATCHa scheduler, not a gate

Near-unanimous: "whatever's ready" has no stable boundary to plan or attest to. It's the right execution model (the Conductor frontier / bd ready --claim) operating within a frozen scope — not the HITL unit. Promoting it re-creates the Hermes failure mode (rejected, DEC-031 D7).

The verdict — and the tradeoff to name Execution = story-grained continuous DAG dispatch (settled). The HITL unit is the live question. Majority = SPRINT (defensible on the integration-AVFL argument, but partly sunk-cost). The tradeoff you should decide deliberately, not inherit: optimize for one coherent integration-verified evaluation (sprint, heavy front-loaded planning) — or for low-per-gate-load, just-in-time, worktree-held story approvals (story, with integration coverage moved to a separate periodic sweep)?

04Preserved dissents — the threads worth pulling before you commit

The bundling dissent (strongest, under-developed). The council fused "integration-review grain" and "planning grain" into one object called "the sprint," then declared the bundle inevitable. The only load-bearing reason sprint beats story is cross-story integration AVFL — but that need not coincide with the planning gate. A rolling integration sweep over the last N merged stories preserves coverage without a frozen sprint container — giving you low-per-gate-load worktree-held story approvals AND removing the 2–8-story planning complexity you flagged. No one developed this. It should be developed before the conduct rewrite is storied.
The epic-as-unit dismissal rests on a false premise (verified). DEC-034 defines finite-lived epics as forward-looking closeable deliverables with acceptance conditions. For small (3–5 story) epics, epic-as-unit ≈ small-sprint-as-unit — and the epic framing is strictly better (it carries a real acceptance_condition). Deserves a genuine hearing.
A static report may beat a live canvas. A live, filterable canvas invites skimming; a static, linear, build-error-enforced report you must read top-to-bottom may force the attention DEC-031 D1 demands. The canvas is the decided means; legibility is the end. If porting the report schema to the canvas weakens its "missing-field = build error" guarantee, the canvas looks legible but reintroduces the original failure.
"Fragments attention" is asserted, not evidenced. Every position rejecting story-at-a-time repeats it with no evidence that total attention exceeds reconstructing one integrated multi-story diff. DEC-031 D1's actual diagnosis — humans fast-track through what they can't genuinely understand — cuts the other way: a 2–8-story diff is harder to understand than one story.
Option E was never costed, including by the self-described skeptic. If the bottleneck really is artifact legibility (not dispatch), the conduct rewrite may be automation work dressed as legibility — and the cheapest honest test of the governing principle was never priced.
