# Retro & Feedback-Loop Lens — Nornspun Delivery Discovery

**Date:** 2026-08-02
**Role:** Does the practice's own feedback loop (momentum:retro) see the "no working user-facing feature" problem, and does it act on what it sees?

---

## Executive Summary

- **The retro for the sprint under investigation (sprint-2026-07-13) never ran.** No `retro-transcript-audit.md`, no `sprint-summary.md`, no ledger entries with `source: retro`, no story-status transitions to `done`. The sprint is still `status: "active"`, `locked: true` in `.momentum/sprints/index.json`, and all 12 of its stories still show `status: "review"` in `.momentum/stories/index.json` — three-plus months after end-gate. The developer's "app doesn't work" discovery therefore happened in a **feedback-loop vacuum**, not a failure of the loop's analysis.
- **The end-gate — not retro — already said this, in plain language, on 2026-07-22.** The sprint-2026-07-13 end-gate report's own hero paragraph reads: *"the sprint's core promise, 'what you tell Urd gets remembered,' is not yet reliable when a real language model drives it."* Three live, adversarially-verified findings (silent capture loss, a capture that saved a fabricated/wrong session's content, and a possible cross-request context-bleed) were escalated to decision cards D4–D6, with a recommended next step: *"make a capture-integrity hardening story the top of next sprint."* That story was never created (verified: no story slug, no intake entry, no ledger entry references it) and no next sprint ever started.
- **Retro's own output has been 100% process-shaped, never product-shaped, across every retro this project has run.** All 7 nornspun retro documents (04-06 → 06-18) that spawn new backlog stories put them in `epic_slug: impetus-core` (pre-2026-05-25) or hand them to the Momentum project's own ledger as `epic_slug: momentum-practice-hardening` / `retro-tooling` (post-2026-05-25, 21+14 items, verified 0 product-epic slugs among them). When a retro narrates a genuine product-level gap (e.g., 06-18's campaign-scoping invariant violation), it references a **pre-existing** end-gate/intake-sourced stub rather than minting new product work itself.
- **None of nornspun's own 29 impetus-core (process-improvement) stories have ever been completed.** 24 are `backlog`, 5 are `obsolete` (superseded, not shipped), 0 are `done`. This includes three stories the 2026-05-01 retro marked `critical` priority specifically to stop "stories marked done while the app shows only errors on screen" — `e2e-pre-flight-gate`, `avfl-fixer-rules-first-and-autonomous`, `e2e-grading-live-evidence-and-targeted-reruns` — all still sitting in backlog as of 2026-08-02, unselected across four subsequent sprints.
- **The retro loop diagnosed the developer's exact hypothesis in its own words, three months before this investigation.** Sprint-2026-05-01's retro executive summary: *"the application was marked 'done' while showing only errors on screen"*; *"quality gates check for artifact presence or process completion rather than outcome validity"* — the user's own phrase, quoted in the audit, was **"a status lie."** This is not a blind spot; it is a correctly-diagnosed, correctly-prioritized, never-remediated finding.
- **The same defect class — cross-conversation/cross-session state bleed — recurs from the project's very first sprint to its most recent one.** Sprint-2026-04-06 (the project's first code sprint): *"I switched to Verdandi but it seemed to maintain the same text, output, context as Urd"* (Norn-switching context bleed, found only by the developer's manual testing, missed by 71 automated E2E scenarios). Sprint-2026-07-13 (the most recent): a live E2E run found a capture record containing a **different campaign's fabricated narrative**, with a working hypothesis of *"cross-request context bleed under concurrent load."* Four months and ~300 stories apart, the same failure family surfaces the same way: caught late, by the most expensive/manual layer, never structurally closed.
- **Sprint-2026-07-13 is not a unique lapse — sprint-2026-05-30 also has no retro artifact** (no `retro-transcript-audit.md`, no `sprint-summary.md`) despite being marked `"status": "done"` in `sprints/index.json`. 2 of the ~9 substantive sprints in this project's history never received a retrospective at all.
- **Retro's own upstream/downstream classification (added by the 2026-04-12 retro, per its own recommendation PA-02) has since routed every subsequent nornspun retro's output away from the project it's meant to improve.** The 2026-04-12 retro produced 15 items, **14 of them (93%) targeting Momentum practice artifacts**, batch-handed to a sibling project (`upstream-momentum-findings.md`) with the explicit rationale that they'd "pollute the nornspun backlog" otherwise. This is a structurally sound instinct (practice work belongs in the practice repo) but its effect is that nornspun's own backlog never accumulates product-recovery stories from retro — only from end-gate/conduct/intake, a different, less scrutinized channel.
- **Where practice-improvement work does get built, it happens in a separate project under separate, active development — not in nornspun.** Momentum's own dogfooding sprint (coincidentally also dated 2026-07-13, in the Momentum repo, unrelated to nornspun's sprint of the same name) shows 111 verified findings and 15/15 stories merged — i.e., Momentum-side process hardening is a live, funded activity. Nornspun's own pre-2026-05-25 backlog of process debt was simply never revisited after the routing policy changed.
- **Counter-signal:** the practice's escalation/deferral/honesty machinery is not rubber-stamping — the 07-13 end-gate is a genuinely candid document (it explicitly says approving it "does not declare the memory loop trustworthy") and 46 of 111 findings synthesized in Momentum's own parallel retro were rated KEEP, meaning the pipeline is not uniformly broken. The gap is specifically between **honest disclosure** and **the sprint-to-sprint mechanism that would act on the disclosure** — and that mechanism (retro closing the loop, and sprint-planning picking up the recommended next story) is precisely what did not fire this time.

### 3. The end-gate already disclosed the exact problem the developer later discovered

The sprint-2026-07-13 end-gate report (`.momentum/handoffs/sprint-2026-07-13-endgate-report.html`, rendered 2026-07-22, 460 lines) is unusually candid for an artifact that would otherwise read as "12/12 stories merged, ship it":

> *"All 12 stories built and merged — and the live verification run then caught the thing that matters most: the sprint's core promise, 'what you tell Urd gets remembered,' is not yet reliable when a real language model drives it. Three related live findings (sessions minted without play, captures narrated but never saved, and one capture saved with the wrong content entirely) all sit at the same seam: whether the model actually calls its save tools when it says it did."*

Three of six decision cards (D4, D5, D6) are dedicated to this cluster. D5's title: *"'It is written.' is sometimes a lie — captures narrated but never saved."* D6's title: *"One capture saved the wrong session entirely — and replies showed other conversations' content."* D6's body explicitly raises the possibility that this is *"a session-isolation failure: the strongest finding class there is, and it must be settled before any multi-user future."* The report's own closing summary table states under "Capture reliability under a live model (D5/D6)": *"Until the hardening story lands, treat 'It is written.' as unverified — a narrated capture may not be saved, and one saved record was outright wrong. This is the gap that matters."* It also discloses, in its own words, that the returning-player greeting is broken: *"Reopening an existing campaign cold still shows the first-meeting greeting; the ratified re-entry design needs infrastructure that doesn't exist yet (stubbed)."*

The report's recommended path (Option A on both D5 and D6): *"Approve the merge as-is, and make a capture-integrity hardening story the top of next sprint... Fold the isolation investigation into the D5 hardening story as its first task."*

**What happened to that recommendation — traced:**
- Searched `.momentum/stories/index.json` for any slug containing `capture-integrity`, `hardening`, `isolation`, `telemetry`, `fabricat`, `tool-call`, `bleed` → the only matches are `per-user-data-isolation-and-security` and `endgate-environment-isolation-guard`, both pre-existing and unrelated (auth-account isolation and conductor-build sandboxing, respectively — not runtime session/capture isolation).
- Searched `.momentum/practice-ledger.jsonl` and `.momentum/intake-queue.jsonl` for the same terms → no match.
- **Conclusion: the single highest-severity, security-adjacent finding of the entire sprint (`build-ledger.jsonl` line 152, `stakes_class: "high-blast-radius-architecture"`, `timing_tier: "end-gate-expanded"`) was escalated, disclosed candidly to the developer, given an explicit recommended remediation — and then never converted into a backlog item, because the mechanism that would normally do that (retro, or a next sprint-planning cycle) never ran.**

The HTML gate itself has no server-side capture of the checkbox/radio selections rendered in it (`paint()` is client-side JS only) and no companion "gate-decision.md" or sign-off record file exists anywhere in the repo — so there is no evidence the six decisions were ever formally ratified, only that the report was committed to the repo.

### 6. The meta-loop's self-improvement backlog is fully stalled

`.momentum/stories/index.json`, filtered to `epic_slug: impetus-core` (29 stories, all created by retro/practice-hardening activity before the 2026-05-25 routing-out policy): **24 backlog, 5 obsolete, 0 done.** This includes every critical-priority fix the practice has ever proposed for itself, including the three from finding #1 above. Zero have ever been selected into a sprint's story list in this project's history (cross-checked against the 12-story 07-13 list, the 9-story 06-18 list, and the 7-story 05-01 list — none contain an `impetus-core` slug).

This is consistent with, but sharper than, the developer's framing of "process work generating process work": in nornspun's own backlog, the practice doesn't even consume itself — the self-diagnosed fixes simply sit unselected. The place where practice work *does* visibly get built is the separate Momentum repository (its own 2026-07-13 dogfooding sprint — a different sprint under the same date, in a different project — shipped 15/15 stories with 111 audited findings). So the meta-loop is not "consuming" nornspun's sprint capacity in the sense of displacing product stories sprint-over-sprint; it is instead **externalized to a sibling project and then never revisited in the original project**, leaving nornspun's own long-standing process debt (including the three critical E2E/AVFL reliability fixes from May) permanently unaddressed in the repo where the product symptom actually occurs.

## Counter-evidence & Falsifiability

Evidence that cuts against the strongest reading of the above, and what would overturn each finding:

- **The pipeline is not a rubber stamp.** The 07-13 end-gate is unusually honest by design (anti-rubber-stamp verdicts, honesty tables, explicit "this does not declare the memory loop trustworthy" disclaimer) and the parallel Momentum-side retro for its own sprint (same date, different project) shows a real 46/41/24 keep/fix/investigate split with zero findings "overturned as factually wrong" — the finding/verification discipline itself is not the failure. **This would be overturned if** a close reading of the actual escalation/disposition events showed findings being silently dropped rather than honestly disclosed-then-orphaned; I did not find evidence of the former, only the latter.
- **Some retro-identified product gaps did get fixed via non-retro channels.** The campaign-init wizard removal (finding #7) shipped and is `done`. This shows the overall system is not incapable of closing a product-architecture gap — it did, once, via a developer-initiated cleanup story rather than via retro's own output. **This would be strengthened or weakened by** checking whether other "shipped" recoveries exist that I didn't have time to trace (see Open Questions) — if several more exist, "retro can't produce product fixes" would need softening to "retro doesn't, but the practice sometimes recovers product fixes through other channels at some rate."
- **An 11-day gap between end-gate (Jul 22) and today (Aug 2) is not, by itself, abnormal** for this project's historical sprint cadence (gaps of 19+ days appear between other sprint pairs, e.g. 04-12→05-01, 05-30→06-18). The anomaly is not the elapsed time — it is that, unlike every other transition, this one shows no retro artifact and no `status: done` transition at all. **This would be overturned if** a retro session was in fact run but its output was written somewhere I didn't check (see Open Questions) or is pending in an uncommitted working state.
- **The retro mechanism, when it runs, is thorough and self-critical**, including auditing its own tooling failures (e.g., 06-18's retro found that 54.1% of its own agent-summary audit trail was unparseable, and flagged it). This is evidence of a functioning critical faculty, not a rubber-stamp process — the gap identified in this lens is about **what retro is scoped to write to** and **whether it runs at all**, not about the quality of its analysis when it does run.
